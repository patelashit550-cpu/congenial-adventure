## Summary

Here, I introduce the social network as we know it today. It emerges fundamentally out of the need for safety, which lies in numbers. Consequently, networks grow much like empires challenging each other for dominance and scaling outward—a dynamic known as Metcalfe's Law. Within this expanding topology, distinct nodes develop. These act as landmarks or Schelling points where we gather because their notable, widely recognized characteristics or personalities hold broad appeal. With our patronage to them, whether in the form of a tax to a sovereign or the purchase of a product, capital accrues to those whose networks have depth. This is the Matthew Effect.

This mass appeal imbues nodes with a quality—diversity—as they provide access to a rich variety of culture. In proximity to them, we cultivate sophistication and an openness to other perspectives. As well, our quieter or weirder aspects—those bits about us we generally keep to ourselves—will find in this much larger milieu others with similarly divergent inclinations, a left-handedness in other things. This is the benefit, for example, of simply living in an urban versus a rural community.

In due consideration, however, much is notionally extracted, for much the same reason that cities are generally more expensive to live in than the countryside. Even as some things pull us into their orbit, so too may we feel repelled by other things. Cities, for example, are often callous, unfriendly places. People barely make eye contact on their crowded streets, and they seem to be set out purely for business. So, we feel both attracted to and repelled by these nodes.

Our response is to curate our space. Where we once dwelled on Facebook or Instagram with most of the world, we migrate instead to curated communities of people we see regularly and with whom we share a vision, perspective, or values on things such as political economy. Perhaps Telegram.

Through this process—captured by Reed's Law—true communities develop. Transcending traditional geo-spatial boundaries, they are instead bounded by semantic perimeters: a shared code or protocol of being and belonging that binds individuals together in a space where they may feel completely at ease. That protocol is, in fact, a self-evident proof of stake. A space, whether virtual or physical, in which the individual is a stakeholder and belongs in the truest sense without having to include everyone.

What appears as a kaleidoscopic pattern (anarchy) to some and entropic complexity (chaos) to others develops over time into a genuinely appreciative pluralism—one characterized by integral strength, rather than mere (fault) tolerance, as alienation is replaced by the set and setting of tribe. All that is required from the great mass of humanity is simple civility and respect on the highways and byways where we transit to and from our communities.

The emergence of true community enables the discovery of calling and completes our journey to find meaning.

This path or process, however, is arduous and emotionally painful; felt as a genuine crisis in the sense that it demands a chrysalis-like transformation for both the individual and the social network. It is necessary, however—if we are to persist—because only through this evolution will we find the alignment between self, community, and ecology required to make peace with (our) nature.

## Embodiment

The social network is organic; comprised of our individual identities transacting on it like cells in our body coming together to make you work. This is why we often refer to a social network as a body politic and one which the increasing ethno-cultural diversity of our neighbourhoods make global.

We may appear to have agency, freedom, and autonomy—and many of us who are fortunate to live in relatively free societies to some degree actually do. Yet, at the same time, participation on this social network today often seems to dictate that we surrender that autonomy and appreciate an obvious boundedness to our reality dictates at the very least by natural processes. As we depart from that minimal natural boundedness into for lack of a better term, civilization, even in our prosperity, many of us feel as though we are merely subsisting or surviving due to the stifling nature of added various constraints: taxation, bureaucracy, dress codes and other grate expectations ad infinitum. Society then comes to feel like a slicer and dicer - a mechanism by which we are processed and shared in some grand institution, a Leviathan, for others to consume.

Many of us, too, feel inexorably pulled by the common will of this body politic, a *Leviathan*, to behave in certain ways, our actions subtly steered by engagement-driven algorithms. We have become part of a collective metabolism where our attention and data are harvested to feed the network's growth—through profit which accrues to the giants who bestride them.

I don't say that this is a bad thing necessarily. Contentment and survival are not mandatory either for us as individuals or as a collective and as a species. The opportunity to live lives infused with autonomy if not utter freedom is there for the taking but it must be seized. Nor is this something we can ask others to do for us. We do it ourselves and thereby set the example. Now my own sense is that humanity has a destiny and that really a capacity to assume a responsibility for this planet is an entrance exam for a much larger responsibility - the Universe; and even if that cannot be falsified it at least models an inspirational narrative as we try to come together. 

For now let's keep our feet firmly on the ground in the rigamarole that has become our twenty-first century schizoid life - bifurcated between work and the remains of the day.

What I divine is that we are at a stage in our development which will lead to the custody by each of us of our identity as a stack of transactions; a tell (or a tel) with an account in greater or lesser balance with which to furnish our needs as the cumulative effect of our behavior. This semantic perimeter of identity embodied (_Semper Idem_) will be our persona on the network. It will be a thing of enormous value and possibly a way **to harvest** due consideration for micro-transactions that accrue value to the broader world and nudge us through such incentives to collectively govern ourselves both on the network and the real world; indeed, this data is already the source of revenue for firms such as Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon.

As these firms come under pressure for extractive behaviour (monetizing the data they gather about their users) and struggle with moderating and governing interactions on their platforms, ownership of this data will transfer to where it belongs; with the identities responsible and also *accountable* for interactions.

These _Semper Idem_ will be a privately secured embodiment of self publicly emanating a gossip protocol—sharing only the minimal, contextual data required in the immediate moment—much the same way as your mere presence signals something about who you are to those around you.

This ambient conversation connects the node to the network, serving as a vital sign or pulse. Yet, it also provides finely calibrated data concerning affinity and intent, allowing identities to cohere or counterpoise, to attract and differentiate in equal measure. Guided by innate qualities, we navigate this nodality to find genuine meaning while securing the social network—creating utility for ourselves and for the commons to be productive or, if you prefer, to create. This is how we will *flow* together.

This is quite simply so that we may each hold one another accountable, not just for how we treat one another but how we care for *ourselves* as we endeavor simultaneously to custody our global commons and bequeath it to coming generations without being so extractive and without emitting so much in the way of negative externalities.

So much for my ideas concerning the future of human civilization. Let's examine some of the ways in which natural mechanisms akin to those in the physical sciences suspend or keep buoyant if you prefer, our social network. It has much to do with human nature and our desire at once to be both a part of some group and as well simultaneously stand out (but paradoxically through that divergent attribute contribute to the group).


## Data Seance

Networks are primal and elementary. We have had them from the very beginning of life itself - anima. They are auto-generative and seem to come out of nowhere. They emerge through a transmission of all kinds of information not just through appearances, language and costume but as well other subtle cues. These are often imperceptible and even signaled subconsciously. *Pinged* from one person to another, their various fragments may then emanate *further* into the social network. Both acknowledgement of familiarity is established and so too are borders of absolute exclusion drawn. People may even find themselves excluded or welcomed in a place or context without really knowing why.

This in turn helps us define who belongs and who doesn't based entirely on the contextual risk they present. Simultaneously, subtle cues may be emitted to frame notional perimeters or boundaries within which we may define good behavior—all without the palaver of having everyone read and sign onerous consent forms, pledge allegiance to some random piece of cloth, bow to so-called self-evident truths, or appease customs officials at borders.

We see this in the analog world. Experienced bartenders and police officers are very adept at profiling. Similarly, special operations teams are very good at assessing the risk posed in a given environment. These are skills we have begun to lose as our capacity, desire, and opportunity to contemplate or concentrate are challenged by the sheer quantity of noise on our social networks.

However, in the age of digital high-fidelity information, we can instead pass between us any number of factors to clearly position us within a spectrum or perimeter defining our receptivity or acceptability (trust) to one another; we may then operate with ever less friction and ever more ease. Both the monetary and emotional costs of living in complex human systems decline, and so too, therefore, does the burden placed on our ecology.

You will easily discern parallels here between these ideas and various philosophical and spiritual traditions: the flow of _ki_, notions of Hindu karma, and the Stoic philosophy of _amor fati_—as well as Zen notions of contemplation and Christ-consciousness—all of which posit that ease comes from willful *contemplative* surrender; this results in a constant and committed relationship with things and people; a stream of alternating introspection and introversion as cyclical as breath; *and* ultimately alignment between one's identity, humanity and our ecology.
 
Our current models and systems—the ones we map onto ecology and *call* reality, human civilization—feel misaligned. Current affairs is a litany of bad news and that may well be down to media outlets looking for what resonates most here in Canada and the West generally - *other people's* misery - which is one way journalism can generate attention in a way that only bad news can; while *still* making people feel relatively secure and comfortable; something that the governments, corporations and their leaders desire as a singular priority since it drives important datapoints such as consumer confidence, revenue and *future* forecasts for revenue, jobs and so-called growth - as well as election prospects.

This situation seems somewhat bewildering. Even more bizarre appear the protestations concerning the efficacy of liberal democracy and the threats to it from outside what we call the dollar regime. 

Because the single biggest piece of evidence concerning prosperity and therefore security and risk in the long run is the climate. Because contrary to what you may have been told, the long run at least from a human perspective is now and the misalignment is surely the data on a slow-burning train wreck that is the degradation of our soil, air and water - and our neglect concerning the profoundly disturbing reality that is climate change. 

Even as we distract ourselves with conversations around diversity, gender, justice and reproductive choice, our inability to reach a consensus on this foundational issue and instead argue about details such as the transference of intellectual property and cash is the source of my *own* alienation as well as the single most important driver concerning how I make every personal decision to expend energy or money - and the commission or omission of every personal action.

## Network Effect

In a state of nature, having friends helps. You know this if you’ve been out in the backcountry with inclement weather rolling in: everything becomes easier by an order of magnitude in good company. Gathering wood, rigging tarps, filtering water—comfort comes quickly when facilitated by camaraderie, and soon you’re watching the weather roil by. Risk is settled in your favor when you have reliable company, but this requires self-evident commitment. You have to be useful, or at least fun to be around, or have a dogged willingness to learn. Recreation is creative precisely because it carries risk; at the edges, if you aren't careful, a tenuous hold on reality spirals into calamity. When you are mindfully operating in a collaborative network however, the *net* work or toil is reduced and productivity is actually felt (as enjoyment or fun). And isn't that, when you think about it, apart from the bare necessities, what we are willing to pay for?

Security, then, requires little more than trust founded on ability and the easy banter that makes joint effort flow. Soon, roles spontaneously develop as a division of labor naturally generates alchemy. Everyone's pack gets lighter. Most of all, all this relies on a simple social contract: carry your own weight, or at least make a gestural effort as a form of *essential* gratitude. If the trip would be more enjoyable without you, next time it will happen without you because the wrong company reduces both the margin for error and enjoyment.

This matters in the broader social network because comfort in the wilderness has nothing to do with money. Capital, yes certainly—the gear and technology and the know-how to use it well—but not *fungible* forms of money. In the wild, _you_ are your money. On the social network however cash *is* relevant.

Many of us carry a broad range of key ambitions—activities and achievements from which we expect to generate an ongoing sense of contentment. Some are felt innately and are particular to our personality, while others are based on expectations set in society as time-tested ways in which the people have found meaning or at least value. The two sets will dovetail and diverge in ways that are unique and shaped by how conservative or adventurous we each are while outcomes are based on how we meet whatever options are available to us in the real world. 

Most of us have at least at the outset the following as objectives: An education or acquisition of skills (formal and/or didactic); becoming homeowners and financially committed parents; intimacy, friendship and play. However the *centerpiece* of it all, the engine that fuels these priorities for us living in complex modern civilization, and we're not in the backcountry, is a job. 

For some—hopefully many—a job becomes a vocation; something more like a vacation or *holiday*. than the toil often associated with work. This is because some task or set of responsibilities align with a deep interest. Our curiosity is piqued; something captures our attention and forces an inward contemplation following which we reach more *deeply* into that task or responsibility. Anything worth doing is surely worth doing for free; and yet here we are drawing a salary for the pleasure of doing this thing. Gratitude manifests and so too joy as are then compelled to explore it further. That connectedness and flow is exhilarating. It may even be said that we are are in vocation. This is when we are optimally creative or productive and feel *most* alive rather than mostly alive.

Even if we aren't able to direct affairs in a way that would generate this productivity frequently enough to satisfy us, we at least derive *symbolic* capital such as wealth, title, or particularly notable achievements; things which are appreciated broadly as being *social* capital. This may then lead to new opportunities that may resonate. This is how personal growth happens: when a vocation becomes a sprawling journey but which has a clear sense of direction and purpose; as though paved stone by stone with intention imbued with meaning.

*Institutional* careers, jobs in government, academia or at large firms have had broad appeal since they offered security (often referred to as tenure). These however entail a certain degree of compromise. Hierarchies require compliance to administrative processes. The ease created by these governance by forms constrains agency and creativity. The compromise of compromise too is that the tenure and progression demanded to allow a flow of new talent requires a *balance* of collaboration *and* competition as employees compete against each other for ever-shrinking pool of leadership positions.

This then requires a form of politics as one applies a deliberate bias favoring one's own interests over those of others which may then in turn lead to the development of factions. In many liberal western democracies these factions have actually hardened into persistent parties.


## Company Benefits

This isn't *necessarily* to the benefit of *any* institution: it *would* prefer all else equal outcomes that are *transparently* defensible - these are also generally the optimal ones. Indeed the whole idea of KPIs (*key* performance indicators) concerns the matter of rolling performance indices up the corporeal hierarchy so as to assess what is and is not working *productively*. Alternatively some sort of mechanism that assesses the 'downward' investment of capital against upward generation of yield. Emergent proof of stake protocols can do just that through on-chain self-sovereign identity. While yet allowing talent to express to the rest of the world how they in particular are responsible - since attributable value is expressed on chain much like a soul-bound token in gaming. You claimed a salary so you wouldn't take those tokens with you. Your clan wouldn't appreciate that but your relative credibility, a deterministic account from which trust may be inferred is still express That's what matters.

This sets up an interesting challenge based on incumbent frameworks which are still somewhat analog: not just off-chain and proprietary but also subject to legal terms that allow considerable discretion on what a company *needs* to disclose concerning its employees, former employees, vendors and contractors. They will naturally disclose only what they are legally bound to do so; generally the balance weighs in the favour of company simply because of their heft in a market democracy. Such is the reality of political economy versus the theoretical and abstracted free market economics modeled by Paul Samuelson et. al. the better part of a century ago; that in a world where a corporation has legal status as an person, in purely utilitarian terms the interests of a single person who is actual human doesn't really count for much.

There's not much one can do but accept that reality as an imperfect situation and one to be improved upon if possible. For an ordinary Canadian or Briton it's at least an improvement over the Del Monte and Chiquita economics of a Central American country (whose poor bargaining position is the origin of the Yankee slur *Banana* Republic) or say the corporate governance on display during the 1980s in Bhopal India where sloppy compliance led to the death of thousands of ordinary workers.

The stakes are not nearly so high in the beige offices where we do so-called white collar work. At the most legal entities here are concerned with protecting their creative kernel -  copyright, patent and the top talent in whose hands it works the best; if necessary with non-compete provisions but where possible generous salaries, allowances and performance-index equity options.

This is why we see arising alienation within our institutions. The malaise concerning government, academia and journalism is well-covered but where it really hit home is the limited liability corporation. 

A firm's revenue in a given period is finite and an ever greater portion of the firm's income is devoted to paying its senior board-facing coterie. Internal income inequality within the corporation is best illustrated by a deteriorating Gini coefficient - a measure wherein a 1:1 ratio implies perfect equality with all employees earning the same salary: in 1965, a principal executive earned roughly 21 times the average wage but by 2024, that ratio had surged to 285:1. The extreme outlier in this trend is Starbucks, where the CEO’s compensation once reached a staggering 6,666 times the pay of the average employee.

This is an unfolding crisis in fundamental inequity and it results in the defection from all of societies core institutions in a disorderly and inchoate fashion. My own sense is that economic activity is scarcely differentiated from other aspects of life as we return to creating product in guild like fashion as communities of interest emerge.

However the evolution to that distant vision or model is only just beginning and our institutions will need to begin adopting emergent protocols that will facilitate this transition or even just allow these institutions to persist on a sounder financial footing. The insight of transparent flow is genuinely creative and to be heralded not feared.

They are also at the core of modern liberal theory concerning political economy - the secular faith, apologia, or belief system we call *market* democracy (often and in my opinion mistakenly referred to as capitalism).

## Pillars of Salt

In the liberal west three sets of interests form the *intellectual* foundation of both the (modern) liberal and (traditional) realist schools of political economy as a way to model how an actor expresses power. Fungible power (or coercive force) is becoming untenable even by large states because of its cost in reputation and brand terms while non-fungible power (influence) grows as mechanism to effect change by the individual. Traditionally the actors wielding non-fungible power were:

- **Firms** through the ethical-framing of the idea focusing on revenue above all else
- **Managers** who are typically not themselves alone material owners of a firm in the broad economy but beholden to passive shareholders
- **Governments** who intervene with fiscal and monetary policy to maintain societal order within their domains

The Austrian School of Economics has luminaries such as Friedrich Hayek and Ludwig von Mises who believed that any kind of central planning should become unnecessary in a pluralist domain, perimeter or jurisdiction in which local knowledge (beginning with an *own* individual's personal choices and preferences) dictate outcomes based on reason and sober intuition. Friedrich Hayek’s most famous concept is *Spontaneous Order*—the optimistic idea that complex systems (like language or markets) are generative and that an anarchic governance emerges from seemingly atomistic individual actions occurring without central planning. The behavioural school of economics focuses on the psychology and the influence applied to manipulate individual decision-making, including financial decisions. These serve as a foundation for understanding the political economy supplanting the classical school which is now reduced to the heuristic that in any given market demand for a product (digital or otherwise) will be met with a supply for it and the choices made by individuals within the constraints imposed by law and social norms. In a pluralist liberal society individual choices will be expressed within a wide range of what is considered normal in society. However it is important to remember that data traverses national boundaries (with their various models for governance) with ease. Information is money ultimately and the most important - the identity as a source from which data emerges will be seen as the unit of measure and the metric used to assess values and trustworthiness in a universally coordinated manner where public reputation becomes either profoundly relevant for an individual or meaningless as a way to measure utility an individual's sense of worth.

Semantic perimeters (communities) grounded in alignment and universal values are now emerging in our networks as ideas are exchanged and gain currency as a trenchant foundation for living, creating, or merely being. However incumbent frameworks remain influential as earnings to pay for their personal living expenses (a salaried income) remain the objective of work for most people regardless of whether they find the work meaningful.



### 1. Firms

The first of those three ideas comes from the economist Milton Friedman who supplied a *moral* justification for maximizing profit. In 1970 Friedman wrote an article for The New York Times Magazine entitled “The Social Responsibility of Business Is to Increase Its Profits”. Its purpose was to *entrench* the limited liability corporation as an engine for innovation while the marketplace delineated which ideas were worth pursuing as products. As for the owners of these firms (increasingly passive shareholders) it was their right to spend their money as they wished; they were after all in the aggregate *consumers* of the same products they were creating.

His success in manufacturing a broad consent for this idea is evidenced by the focus on short-term results or quarterly earnings and a general satisfaction and appreciation for the consumer economy created. However the focus on money versus the product or service for example led to a subtle shift in emphasis; from the product itself to the *earnings* they produced. The idea is expressed by a quote attributed to Thomas Murphy (1915–2006), GM's Chairman and CEO during the 1970s:

> "General Motors is not in the business of making cars. General Motors is in the business of making money." In the tension between earnings and product it generally has been earnings that have won.

Initially mass production was an innovation because it scaled production *without* compromising quality; not because it enabled profits per se but because it made a well-made product more affordable to a larger number. The process by which this became a reality was the assembly line and mass production of product at scale. Implied was the idea that manufacturing requires trade-offs between the necessary and the unnecessary in the product architecture involving how the qualities of a product and its price are optimised. Ford drew a hard line and since everything looked good in minimalist black that was the *only* colour his cars were made available in so capital may be focused elsewhere such as research and development.



### 2. Managers

In order to scale without incurring risk to capital without incurring additional debt the ownership of the firm was diluted to include thousands of ordinary people who given the limited liability of the corporation had no stake in the value (quality) of the product and were only interested in owning a share because of its prospects for generating wealth. So now the firm's success, and indeed even its very ability to operate as a going concern, became dependent on its share price.

The *mechanism* for implementing this tenet was explained by Jensen and Meckling in their 1976 paper — *Theory of the Firm*. When ownership (of the founder) separates from the day-to-day management of a firm, a principal-agent problem is created; namely how do owners led by so-called shareholder activists whose primary concern is profit attract, incentivize, and retain reliable loyal administrators focused on stock performance? Compensation became the lever to achieve those higher stock prices.

The shift in emphasis towards revenue has consequences. A firm's revenue in a given period is finite and an ever greater portion of the firm's income is devoted to paying its senior board-facing coterie. Internal income inequality within the corporation is best illustrated by a deteriorating Gini coefficient - a measure wherein a 1:1 ratio implies perfect equality with all employees earning the same salary: in 1965, a principal executive earned roughly 21 times the average wage but by 2024, that ratio had surged to 285:1. The extreme outlier in this trend is Starbucks, where the CEO’s compensation reached a staggering 6,666 times the pay of the average employee. 

Reflecting David Graeber's assertions concerning what he called 'bullshit jobs', as much as 50% of staffing is related to purely administrative tasks, particularly in Finance and Pharma where the need to manage compliance risk is greatest. The following data are for illustrative purposes only.

The distinction between creative and bureaucratic work is arbitrary—and a model used primarily for thinking about finding meaning through work. However, the window for true creative agency at large corporations is narrowing as AI is deployed to optimize, and eventually automate increasingly complex workflows. Work at the most sophisticated firms with the best talent empowers small self-governed teams working on products with Scrum and Kanban methods, affording them considerable autonomy.

These trends have led to some soul searching by workers whose skills traverse process engineering and other technical realms as well as content creation rooted in the liberal arts. They are not particularly motivated by higher salaries if it leads to increasingly administrative and political responsibilities far from solving real world problems for customers and instead require the need to compromise integral strength meeting arbitrary deadlines with long hours incurring both personal and professional technical debt - trauma inflicted as well to the network's social architecture. 

That social architecture is the sovereign's domain and in a world in which vast amounts of capital are transferred across national borders and yet still within a corporation's domain this sets up the profoundly difficult work of sorting legitimate from illegitimate behaviour. Namely behaviour that is both legal and ethical. Ultimately the decision to whether work is meaningful however is entirely subjective and the domain of the individual performing it.

### 3. Government

The shift towards knowledge work has changed the way in which the scope of government—regulatory affairs, for example, concerning health and safety (security of the person)—is managed. An increasing proportion of our capital is digital rather than physical. Apart from time spent in the appreciation of aesthetic work (art or design) our attention to things correlates to the value or utility it has. Attention is now the generator of value (increasingly seen as the primary factor driving productivity). Content and access to it by humans versus bots seeking to scan for intelligence and to extract value (but not necessarily apply its logic) will be the ultimate source of logic governing both the network and one's agency as a participant on it and as well determine *social* credit. Digital content may be defined as anything which modulates attention to shape and frame the logic of consumption (which may now be a human, machine, or even just an object). To what end is now the primary objective of governance. If maximal extraction of value is the logic for a credible network then the question for a node (ultimately an end user) is of what value is that extraction to them, their community (the Sovereign) and the global commons for proof of stake communities and how should it be prioritized in a *finite* world? This is the tragedy of the commons all actors ultimately have a stake in solving regardless of ideology, philosophy or other factors motivating *human* behavior.

Whether at work or not we all spend an enormous amount of our time viewing, manipulating and creating digital value. On the internet now as persons with identity we all use the network; we are each visible to the network (and to varying degrees to each other) as an expression of how data is transformed and turned into value. Over time our collective attention aggregates; optimal paths develop between points on a network (users) to facilitate conservation of resources (time and energy). Over time the network generates a form of neural intelligence that operates in the interests of all its participants through the combination of content (memory) and the optimal pathways to them.
