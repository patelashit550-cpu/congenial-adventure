## Safety First

Here, I introduce the social network as we know it today. It emerges fundamentally out of a need for *safety*, which lies in numbers. Consequently, networks grow much like empires challenging each other for dominance and scaling outward out to reduce insecurity—a dynamic known as Metcalfe's Law. Within this expanding topology, distinct nodes develop. At least initially these are individual identities; each acting like a landmark around which we gather because of its notable, widely recognized characteristics. It has some sense of culture or personality and holds broad appeal. Our patronage allows these nodes to extract a levy or some continuous revenue and so develop a deep well, or mound of capital of capital. This accrual of capital is referred to sometimes at the Matthew Effect.

The mass appeal of a node comes from its character. It may be said to be cultured. In the first instance sense culture is organic in the sense that bacteria or a cancer *is* a culture and growth it's very purpose. In the second instance it is a utility. Basic physiological needs must be met before any higher-level cultural expression can exist. In the final abstract instance culture abstracts and encodes this utility as an expression of ideas. Philosophy, mythology or perhaps even some sort of sport - and the rituals around it (whether it is primal and in pursuit of actual game or not).

A node is a person, or today with the emergence of information networks it may be some thing which has the ability to receive inputs and generate outputs such that *ease* *or* *comfort* is generated. Life is made easier and time becomes available for whatever one wishes to use it for - whether that be play or contemplation or some other entirely enjoyable pursuit; that which does not entail any toil - at least to extent that it is perceived as suffering.
 
Nodes make a reckoning of the world. They take a true account of it and communicate this with the rest of the network in an abstracted sense. As if to say: Here is what I have assumed for myself and here is what I generate from it as a yield.
 
In proximity to diverse and open nodes, we cultivate sophistication and an openness to other perspectives. As well, our introverted aspects—those bits about us that we generally keep to ourselves—will find in this much larger milieu community. Therefore *networks* develops around them. 

Our most significant nodes - corporate entities, academic institutions, governments, the media and even the professions and professionals associated with each extract much in due consideration for what they provide. They also generate what colloquially may be referred to a lot of heat and noise; an inchoate, inconsistent and conflicting signal; a general dissonance and degradation of environment. In short they are not functioning as nodes ought to; by becoming *more* productive over time. The purpose of information technology is to *lower* prices over time and generate ease otherwise it's not really a utility. Instead, we see prices continuously rising.

Consequently, trust in them has declined precipitously. Those who work in these domains respect them, and may even admire their capacity to harvest a return and thus secure their own sinecures, but few would say they comprehensively trust them. Persistently high employee turnover sees this sentiment reciprocated; we therefore default to a zero-trust posture entailing a high degree of pressure and anxiety.

Those of individuals on the network who are not criminal (anywhere nor in any sense) but are not party to the dominant nodes are the primary legitimate source of revenue. They make the network not only credibility but viable. So it will be a concern to the regime that many independent thinkers are not participating in the public discourse but rather choosing to tightly define their own semantic perimeters - collectives, guilds, cooperatives - people all essentially see their work as a fundamental aspect of their personality rather than just a job.

Through this process—captured by Reed's Law—true communities develop. Transcending traditional geo-spatial boundaries: a shared code or protocol of being and belonging that binds individuals together in a space where they may feel completely at ease. A space, whether virtual or physical, in which the individual is a stakeholder and belongs in the truest sense without having to include anyone or everyone.

What appears as a kaleidoscopic pattern (anarchy) to some and entropic complexity (chaos) to others as we congregate develops over time into a genuinely appreciative pluralism—one characterized by integral strength, rather than mere (fault) tolerance, as alienation is replaced by the set and setting of tribe where perception is found. All that is required from the great mass of humanity is simple civility and respect on the highways and byways where we transit to and from our communities.

The emergence of community is empowering; it creates the ease and comfort where one can be themselves and divine what it is they possess of real utility; this enables *Calling*.

The path on this journey, however, is emotionally painful because it makes us vulnerable. The instancy of social media, for example, rewards impulsivity; this, combined with the sharing of application data on our devices, means we're putting *a lot* of ourselves out there. Pretty much all of it. Knowing that you are sharing everything and *not minding* is probably the single most important trait to adopt in this age.

This undoubtedly opens each of us up to judgement and opprobrium initially because *most* people are in the groove of the collective, and this is what we need to overcome if we are to bring our divergent *difference* engine properly into gear—the optionality of *beta* or *bravo* praxis to the alpha orthodoxy of the moral majority to enable an open society. It is felt as a genuine crisis in the sense that it demands a chrysalis-like transformation for both the individual and the social network. It is necessary, however—if we are to persist—because only through this evolution will we find the alignment between self, community, and ecology required to make peace with our nature.

## Trust

Networks are primal and elementary. We have had them from the  beginning of life itself. They emerge through the transmission of information not just through appearances, language and costume but as well other subtle cues between two points - we can call them people, identities or nodes. These are often imperceptible and even signaled subconsciously initially as openness to co-existence and then to a social interaction; each interaction building, one upon another until a relationship is built. 

This relationship may then be seen as a set of rails and the extent to which those rails are used and time invested in them implies an *exchange of something meaningful, substantial and of value*. This exchange of value is *reciprocal* and from this mutuality comes trust.

A certain amount of the information transferred between parties is then persisted; appearances, mannerisms, voice, and eventually details about one another's identity so we're not continually having to set down those rails every time we meet. There is a practical limit to the number of people with whom we may or even wish to establish a meaningful relationship; time is finite and of course we have a relationship with ourselves too in some sense. A degree of self-awareness, confidence and poise is after all a pre-requisite to developing relationships with others and being of value to the network.

Familiarity is acknowledged regularly at a minimum to persist these rails; we stop to speak to those we encounter randomly. So too are borders drawn perhaps by keeping our averting our gaze or feigning distraction as we pass others.

The willingness to invest in relationships comes from the possibility of gain. Today what many of us who are well past the idea of fundamental scarcity seek are new perspectives and original ideas - particularly as it pertains to some commonly experienced problem. We hold fast to someone who offers original and interesting insights and hope they will share more. Through them perhaps we reach other interesting individuals and are introduced to community of expertise; these become our favorite trails in the augmented reality we superimpose onto society as a social network.

Meanwhile is the lighter mesh of the frequently encountered acquaintances on trains or cafes or supermarkets whose familiarity provides a level of comfort. There are too various proxies - unique aspects of our regional or national culture that makes us feel more at ease when we're home than in a neighboring country even if the same language is spoken there.

At the most fundamental level what make a human system a social network is the *trust* which serves as its foundation. Without it we atomize into corners and keep to ourselves. When we lose trust in systems institutions which may be viewed as a cluster of identities or its mechanisms for change (money), we avoid using them. It is *trust* which allows people to cooperate and function in even the harshest conditions without money and without the institutions of the retail politician.

## Collaboration

In a state of nature, having friends helps. You know this if you’ve been out in the backcountry with inclement weather rolling in: everything becomes easier by an order of magnitude in good, i.e. competent company. Gathering wood, rigging tarps, filtering water—comfort comes quickly when facilitated by camaraderie which follows from collaboration; soon the weather is irrelevant. Risk is settled in your favor when you have reliable company, but this requires self-evident commitment. You have to be useful, look as though you're enjoying yourself, and display a willingness to learn. Recreation is creative precisely because it carries risk; at the edges, if you aren't careful, a tenuous hold on reality spirals into calamity. When you are mindfully operating in a collaborative network, however, the *net* work or toil is reduced and productivity is actually felt (as enjoyment or fun). And isn't that, when you think about it, apart from the bare necessities, what we are willing to pay for?

Security, then, requires little more than trust founded on ability and the easy banter that makes joint effort flow. Soon, roles spontaneously develop as a division of labor naturally generates alchemy. Everyone's pack gets lighter. Most of all, all this relies on a simple social contract: carry your own weight, or at least make a gestural effort as a form of *essential* gratitude. If the trip would be more enjoyable without you, next time it will happen without you because the wrong company reduces both the margin for error and enjoyment.

This matters in the broader social network because comfort in the wilderness has nothing to do with money. Capital, yes certainly—the gear and technology and the know-how to use it well—but not *fungible* forms of money. In the wild, _you_ are your money. On the social network, however, cash *is* relevant.

Many of us carry a broad range of key ambitions—activities and achievements from which we expect to generate an ongoing sense of contentment. Some are felt innately and are particular to our personality, while others are based on expectations set in society as time-tested ways in which the people have found meaning or at least value. The two sets will dovetail and diverge in ways that are unique and shaped by how conservative or adventurous we each are while outcomes are based on how we meet whatever options are available to us in the real world.

Most of us have at least at the outset the following as objectives: an education or acquisition of skills (formal and/or didactic); becoming homeowners and financially committed parents; intimacy, friendship and play. However the *centerpiece* of it all—the engine that fuels these priorities for us living in complex modern civilization, when we are not in the backcountry—is a job.

For some—hopefully many—a job becomes a vocation; something more like a vacation or *holiday* than the toil often associated with work. This is because some task or set of responsibilities align with a deep interest. Our curiosity is piqued; something captures our attention and forces an inward contemplation following which we reach more *deeply* into that task or responsibility. Anything worth doing is surely worth doing for free; and yet here we are drawing a salary for the pleasure of doing this thing. Gratitude manifests and so too joy as we are then compelled to explore it further. That connectedness and flow is exhilarating. It may even be said that we are in vocation. This is when we are optimally creative or productive and feel *most* alive rather than mostly alive.

Even if we aren't able to direct affairs in a way that would generate this productivity frequently enough to satisfy us, we at least derive *symbolic* capital such as wealth, title, or particularly notable achievements; things which are appreciated broadly as being *social* capital. This may then lead to new opportunities that may resonate. This is how personal growth happens: when a vocation becomes a sprawling journey but which has a clear sense of direction and purpose; as though paved stone by stone with intention imbued with meaning.

*Institutional* careers, jobs in government, academia or at large firms have had broad appeal since they offered security (often referred to as tenure). These, however, entail a certain degree of compromise. Hierarchies require compliance to administrative processes. The ease created by these governance by forms constrains agency and creativity. The compromise of compromise too is that the tenure and progression demanded to allow a flow of new talent requires a *balance* of collaboration *and* competition as employees compete against each other for an ever-shrinking pool of leadership positions.

This then requires a form of politics as one applies a deliberate bias favoring one's own interests over those of others which may then in turn lead to the development of factions. In many liberal western democracies these factions have actually hardened into persistent parties.









However, in the age of digital high-fidelity information, we can instead pass between us any number of factors to clearly position us within a spectrum or perimeter defining our receptivity or acceptability (trust) to one another; we may then operate with ever less friction and ever more ease. Both the monetary and emotional costs of living in complex human systems decline, and so too, therefore, does the burden placed on our ecology.

You will easily discern parallels here between these ideas and various philosophical and spiritual traditions: the flow of _ki_, notions of Hindu karma, and the Stoic philosophy of _amor fati_—as well as Zen notions of contemplation and Christ-consciousness—all of which posit that ease comes from willful *contemplative* surrender; this results in a constant and committed relationship with things and people; a stream of alternating introspection and introversion as cyclical as breath; *and* ultimately alignment between one's identity, humanity and our ecology.

Our current models and systems—the ones we map onto ecology and *call* reality, human civilization—feel misaligned. Current affairs is a litany of bad news and that may well be down to media outlets looking for what resonates most here in Canada and the West generally—*other people's* misery—which is one way journalism can generate attention in a way that only bad news can; while *still* making people feel relatively secure and comfortable; something that the governments, corporations and their leaders desire as a singular priority since it drives important datapoints such as consumer confidence, revenue and *future* forecasts for revenue, jobs and so-called growth—as well as election prospects.

This situation seems somewhat bewildering. Even more bizarre appear the protestations concerning the efficacy of liberal democracy and the threats to it from outside what we call the dollar regime.

Because the single biggest piece of evidence concerning prosperity and therefore security and risk in the long run is the climate. Because contrary to what you may have been told, the long run at least from a human perspective is now and the misalignment is surely the data on a slow-burning train wreck that is the degradation of our soil, air and water—and our neglect concerning the profoundly disturbing reality that is climate change.

Even as we distract ourselves with conversations around diversity, gender, justice and reproductive choice, our inability to reach a consensus on this foundational issue and instead argue about details such as the transference of intellectual property and cash is the source of my *own* alienation as well as the single most important driver concerning how I make every personal decision to expend energy or money—and the commission or omission of every personal action.




The result, after unpacking some serious baggage, is that I am now more content than I have ever been. Much of that contentment comes from recalibrating my relationship to the broader social fabric—particularly with massive institutions like governments, large corporations, and the giants who direct their affairs. Instead, I choose to heed my own sense of ethics, grounded in transparency and aligned with ecology and law. I have discarded those arbitrary, inconsistently applied social norms which align neither with ecology nor basic human ethics, which to my mind should consider as a priority future generations. This is my red line: an orthodox standard that refused to scale in traditionally liberal utilitarian terms. Instead we treat each person encountered as an individual, all the while sensitive to their unique attributes and their particular needs. This is how *I* frame anarchism.

Isolating myself lends itself well to being observant and curious about people when the opportunity *is* there; it's as though I were at a zoo carefully assessing these random creatures on the train or on social media. They seem unhappy, angry and tired. They don't look particularly well either. They express poor posture, stiffness, fatigue and seem to be in a chronic state of profound resistance; as though wishing they were far away; and they usually are—deep into their devices or passed out.

When they are engaged, their voice betrays a readiness to blame others, they get personal or blame our institutions without presenting serious ideas. Often they blame those who lead them as though *they*, in spite of their numerous unmet commitments, were in any position to grapple with climate change or anything else given the shortness of *their* horizons.

As we dwell on this question, let's examine briefly the nature of our social network and its political economy—particularly the qualities which undermine its resilience and its ability to persist. We feel this structural fatigue as the reactive animus prevalent on social media, in local political discourse, and in the defensive posture of Western democratic governments toward their geopolitical adversaries. To understand why this collective nervous system is fraying, we look beneath the mechanics of connection: the physical nature of networks in general—something which needs a degree of warmth to work, one which is maintained in an ambient balance. Something currently missing, and which—judging by the diagram above—we have no reason to feel optimistic; that is to say, any reason to believe it will be arriving presently. All we can do is each one of us hold ourselves accountable until the required mechanisms are in place. This is what makes me an anarchist.

## Sovereign Identity

The social network is organic, comprised of our individual identities transacting on it like cells in our body coming together to make you work. This is why we often refer to a social network as a body politic—and one whose  modern ethno-cultural diversity makes it global.

We may appear to have agency, freedom, and autonomy—and many of us who are fortunate to live in relatively free societies and have accumulated sufficient capital actually do.

Many of us feel inexorably pulled by the body politic's collective will to comply as our actions are subtly steered by engagement-driven algorithms to consume things. We have become part of a collective metabolism where our attention and data are harvested to feed the network's growth—through profit which accrues to giants. Alienation has set in as the constraints of civilization feel ever tighter while leaving us disappointed by its minimum viable culture - something satisfactory but scarcely sustainable.

>Can't you see a giant walks among you  
>Seeing through your petty lives?  
>Do you think I do these things for real?  
>I do these things just so I survive  
>And you know I will survive
>
>~ Pulp (I Spy)

Humanity's destiny is to be accountable for our planet. The criterion is assuming a responsibility for affording a basic standard of care for our ecology and biodiversity. This in my view entails the application of behaviours which in time will govern the social network and achieve balance autonomously through a broad trust-based distribution of capital.

We are at a stage where we may define identity *ourselves* and so fully represent ourselves in a whole way instead of as a collection of fragments distributed across the networks at the various nodes upon it. This semantic perimeter of identity embodied—a *Semper Idem*—would be a tell or a mound of data built almost as though stone by stone by the sequence of our interactions with one another on a transparent but publicly available network; stable yet forever evolving.

Like our wearable devices whose data is analytically filtered to provide actionable insights, so too will on-chain identity. It will allow you to harvest key insights and share them as you decide; information about how you interact with the world; a relationship whose outcomes whether in the particular or aggregate you may wish to improve. These data may furnish institutions with the ability to financially or otherwise incentivize choices that benefit everyone; nudge us into governing ourselves in the real world but through an on-chain social network made of self-sovereign nodes, effective because they are a complete and holistic impression of you. 

For two decades, this data has fueled a Matthew Effect for firms such as Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon, allowing their initial advantages in data aggregation to exponentially compound over time. They use this to preferentially place consumer products and services in front of us and not necessarily in a way that is objectively in our interests as our data traverse the network through *their* centralized nodes. 

Instead each of us as objective *nodes* could allow data to traverse on protocols between us as we assess this data and route it objectively and based on the alignment of our interests; in this way we may then finally get through to each other and resonate where we should rather than just wherever we can in order to get by.

The centralized nodes of today is at least a relative decentralization in that these nodes are at least *de jure* at arm's length from the regime. Seen through the broad lens of history—this is the progress of devolution from the dominance of hegemony; it wasn't too long ago that western governments enforced segregation and arguably they still have some ways to go in enabling equity, never mind individual autonomy and agency. They are as well still prone to using utilitarian justifications for running roughshod over human rights.

Many corporations, particularly the behemoths engaged in the information economy, now wield enough structural gravity to intimidate nation-states, forcing traditional governments into a posture of immediate compliance to whatever tracks in the public zeitgeist.

As they draw our attention and become the conduit for dissonant and discordant voices, they have succeeded wildly in monetizing our attention and engagement. This is known as the Matthew Effect:

>For to everyone who has will more be given, and he will have an abundance. But from the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away.
>— **Matthew 25:29**

You can see in stark terms the Matthew Effect over time as wealth accrues to corporations and other large institutions such as universities. The split between the two groups in terms of population is roughly equal and has kept pace. The 27 million versus 30 million in 1970 had grown to 61 million and 69 million for Main Street and Wall Street, respectively, by 2026. However, looking at the staggering variance in capital allocation, the reality of the divide becomes clear.

## Mechanics of Belonging

Clearly our social network is not distributive and we see that in the broad and persistent disparities in wealth and incomes both within western societies such as Canada and the United States themselves and as well the post-colonial disparity between the North and the South, something exacerbated by persistent geo-political conflict between East and West. These are tectonic pressures of networks at their largest scale as our largest semantic perimeters vie for economic pre-eminence which seeks wealth effects at another's expense instead of examining how their own architecture might be more efficient.

**HERE** If Pascal wagered on God for infinite upside against finite downside, the social network offers a secular analogue: open yourself to the group—risking exposure—and the upside is Reed's exponential belonging; remain closed and what you keep is Metcalfe's thinner graph of mere connection.

We reacted to these platforms with some joy, prospecting for and restoring lost connections—calls whose signal fell away then dropped entirely just because; then at some point as we poked around further some enchanting stranger, a friend of a friend perhaps, might have caught our eye. Soon we opened up to the tantalizing opportunity of making new connections and then finally exploring niche self-moderating communities who minded their own business. This is Reed's Law in action: the gravitational pull of these platforms lay in the intoxicating number of even radical sub-groups that formed for us to express our identity.

To keep these groups *safe*, our online behaviour is used to furnish applications with important information. Much like your physical presence signals cues to the people around you, networked applications rely on an architecture of selective contextual disclosure. Operating via a gossip protocol, they share only the minimal data required in any given moment. In this way applications are in constant conversation as they seek to attest identity and verify us in the places that matter to us—where we want to be—with *our* people. This mechanism enables a truer expression of our digital identity. It clarifies how well our projected public image aligns with reality—verifying not just that we are real humans, but measuring our honesty and trustworthiness. From a utilitarian perspective, this data can even illuminate where we truly belong, placing us where we offer the greatest value to ourselves and our networks.

This is how Reed's Law manifests in the physical world: the true power of the network scales exponentially through the infinite subgroups we can construct. Guided by innate qualities, we navigate this nodality to find genuine meaning in community.

This is by way of how we may hold one another accountable without coercive power; how we treat each other both within and between these communities in the commons.





## Institutional Decay and Protocol

This isn't *necessarily* to the benefit of *any* institution: it *would* prefer all else equal outcomes that are *transparently* defensible—these are also generally the optimal ones. Indeed the whole idea of KPIs (*key* performance indicators) concerns the matter of rolling performance indices up the corporeal hierarchy so as to assess what is and is not working *productively*. Alternatively some sort of mechanism that assesses the 'downward' investment of capital against upward generation of yield. Emergent proof of stake protocols can do just that through on-chain self-sovereign identity, while yet allowing talent to express to the rest of the world how they in particular are responsible—since attributable value is expressed on chain much like a soul-bound token in gaming. You claimed a salary so you wouldn't take those tokens with you. Your clan wouldn't appreciate that, but your relative credibility—a deterministic account from which trust may be inferred—is still expressed. That's what matters.

This sets up an interesting challenge based on incumbent frameworks which are still somewhat analog: not just off-chain and proprietary but also subject to legal terms that allow considerable discretion on what a company *needs* to disclose concerning its employees, former employees, vendors and contractors. They will naturally disclose only what they are legally bound to disclose; generally the balance weighs in the favour of company simply because of their heft in a market democracy. Such is the reality of political economy versus the theoretical and abstracted free market economics modeled by Paul Samuelson et al. the better part of a century ago; that in a world where a corporation has legal status as a person, in purely utilitarian terms the interests of a single person who is actual human doesn't really count for much.

There's not much one can do but accept that reality as an imperfect situation and one to be improved upon if possible. For an ordinary Canadian or Briton it's at least an improvement over the Del Monte and Chiquita economics of a Central American country (whose poor bargaining position is the origin of the Yankee slur *Banana* Republic) or say the corporate governance on display during the 1980s in Bhopal India where sloppy compliance led to the death of thousands of ordinary workers.

The stakes are not nearly so high in the beige offices where we do so-called white collar work. At most, legal entities here are concerned with protecting their creative kernel—copyright, patent and the top talent in whose hands it works the best; if necessary with non-compete provisions but where possible generous salaries, allowances and performance-index equity options.

This is why we see arising alienation within our institutions. The malaise concerning government, academia and journalism is well-covered but where it really hit home is the limited liability corporation.

A firm's revenue in a given period is finite and an ever greater portion of the firm's income is devoted to paying its senior board-facing coterie. Internal income inequality within the corporation is best illustrated by a deteriorating Gini coefficient—a measure wherein a 1:1 ratio implies perfect equality with all employees earning the same salary: in 1965, a principal executive earned roughly 21 times the average wage but by 2024, that ratio had surged to 285:1. The extreme outlier in this trend is Starbucks, where the CEO’s compensation once reached a staggering 6,666 times the pay of the average employee.

This is an unfolding crisis in fundamental inequity and it results in the defection from all of society's core institutions in a disorderly and inchoate fashion. My own sense is that economic activity is scarcely differentiated from other aspects of life as we return to creating product in guild-like fashion as communities of interest emerge.

However the evolution to that distant vision or model is only just beginning and our institutions will need to begin adopting emergent protocols that will facilitate this transition or even just allow these institutions to persist on a sounder financial footing. The insight of transparent flow is genuinely creative and to be heralded, not feared. That flow—when restored to the body politic—is Calling returned to the network.
